# 1.4 — Data Analytics Projects Summary

We've covered quite a bit in this module — from understanding data to analyzing it and sharing our work. So, before moving on, let's connect the dots.

## 1. The Data Analytics Lifecycle

A data analytics project isn't just about opening a dataset and making a few charts. It starts with a question and ends with findings that people can understand and use.

The six steps are:

1. **Asking the question** — Understand the problem you're trying to solve.
2. **Getting the data** — Find and collect the relevant data.
3. **Investigating the data** — Check whether the data is complete, relevant, and suitable for the task.
4. **Preparing the data** — Clean and organize it for analysis.
5. **Analyzing the data** — Look for patterns, relationships, and useful insights.
6. **Presenting the results** — Communicate what you found through reports, charts, or other suitable formats.

One important thing to remember: **this lifecycle is iterative.** You might discover a problem with the data or realize you need to ask a better question. That's okay! You can revisit an earlier step instead of blindly continuing.

## 2. Four ways to think about data

Analytics can answer different kinds of questions:

- **Descriptive analytics:** What happened?
- **Diagnostic analytics:** Why did it happen?
- **Predictive analytics:** What might happen?
- **Prescriptive analytics:** What should we do?

These approaches help turn raw data into insights that support better decisions.

## 3. Understand the problem before analyzing it

A project needs more than data and tools. It needs a clear purpose.

**Business understanding** means understanding the problem, the project's goals, and the needs of the people who will use the results.

For example, if a business notices that sales are falling, simply showing a sales chart might not be enough. It may need to investigate possible reasons, estimate future sales, and consider what actions could help.

The question you ask influences the data you need and the analysis you perform.

## 4. Your work matters — so show it!

Learning a skill is one thing. Demonstrating that you can apply it is another.

A **portfolio** is a collection of work that provides evidence of your skills and capabilities. A useful project should explain the problem, the data, your approach, your findings, and what you learned.

You can share your work in different ways:

- **GitHub:** Store project files, documentation, and code; track changes with Git and collaborate with others.
- **Personal website:** Bring your projects, biography, skills, and other professional information together.
- **Blog:** Share your experiences, explain your approach, and reflect on challenges and lessons learned.
- **Tableau Public:** Showcase interactive data visualizations.

A portfolio and a blog aren't exactly the same. A portfolio focuses on demonstrating your work, while a blog gives you space to explain your thinking and experiences.

## 5. Document your projects properly

A project becomes easier for others to understand when you document it clearly.

A few important terms:

- **Git:** A version control system that tracks changes to files.
- **GitHub:** An online platform for hosting repositories and collaborating on projects.
- **Repository:** A place to organize and store project files.
- **README:** A document that explains what a project is, what it does, and how to understand or use it.

You don't need to build something incredibly complicated to start a portfolio. Even a beginner project can demonstrate useful skills when the work and your learning process are explained clearly.

## My takeaway

The biggest lesson from this module is that data analytics is about more than calculations. It involves asking meaningful questions, working with appropriate data, finding insights, and communicating the results.

And there's another lesson I want to remember as a learner: **don't just learn a concept and leave it in your notes. Try applying it to a project, document what you did, and share it when you're ready.**

Learn it. Apply it. Explain it. Keep improving.

That's how knowledge gradually becomes practical skill.
