# Data Investigation Checklist

When I first started learning data analytics, the different terms and steps could feel like a lot to remember. But when you actually work with a dataset, you can approach it one step at a time.

This checklist brings together the main things to look at when you begin investigating a dataset.

## Before analysing the data

- **Understand the question:** What am I trying to find out?
- **Identify the source:** Where did the data come from?
- **Check relevance:** Does this data help answer my question?
- **Understand the structure:** What does each row represent, and what do the columns describe?

## While investigating the data

- **Identify observations:** What does each record represent?
- **Identify variables:** What characteristics are recorded?
- **Identify values:** What information is stored in each cell?
- **Identify variable types:** Are the variables categorical or numerical?
- **Look for data quality issues:** Are there missing values, duplicates, errors, or inconsistent formats?
- **Check whether more data is needed:** Is the current dataset enough to answer the question?

## Before presenting the findings

- **Choose an appropriate tool:** For example, Excel can help with cleaning, calculations, and summaries.
- **Choose a suitable visualization:** Use a chart that matches the question and the type of data.
- **Interpret the results carefully:** Look for patterns without assuming that a relationship automatically means causation.
- **Connect the findings to the original question:** Explain what the analysis actually tells us.

## My main takeaway

Investigating data isn't just about opening a spreadsheet and immediately creating charts. It's about understanding the problem, checking what information is available, making sure the data is suitable, and deciding how to analyse and present it.

Taking these steps before jumping into conclusions can make the entire analysis more useful and reliable.
